The Lie of Normal

The Gap Between Who You Are In The Room And Who The World Sees

Tiesha Green · The Greatness Revolution™

In the coaching room, you say the real thing.

On the call, in the session, on the stage, the thing that shifts everything comes out of you without effort. Direct. True. Exactly what needed to be said.

Then you open Instagram. Or sit down to write an email. Or get ready to post.

And something happens between your brain and the screen. The words that come out are softer. Safer. Rounder at the edges.

And it doesn't sound like you. And you know it. And you post it anyway, or you don't post at all.

This isn't a content problem. It goes much deeper than that.

Two different people

If this is you, you're carrying two versions of yourself. The one who shows up fully in a room where it's just you and one other person, and the one who shows up in public, watered down, edited, palatable.

Both of those people are real. But only one of them is actually you.

The private version isn't more "you" because it's braver. It's more "you" because there's no audience there to perform normal for.

Everyone is already a brand. The question is which one.

Here's something worth sitting with: everyone is a brand, whether they know it or not, whether they like it or not. The question was never whether you have one. It's whether it's a standout one, or a softened one built to avoid making anyone uncomfortable.

Right now, the public version of you is the softened one. And the people who need the real one can't find them, because they're hidden behind the safe one.

Why this feels so much riskier than it should

You help other people be brave publicly for a living. Which makes this gap feel almost humiliating to admit.

I want to be clear: naming this is not shaming you for it. It's common. It's not a character flaw. It's the exact same conditioning I've talked about before, just showing up in a very specific place: the space between your private voice and your public one.

Playing small in public doesn't feel safe anymore, even if it used to. It just quietly costs you the exact people who were looking for the version of you that says the real thing.

What breaks the loop

Standing out isn't vanity. It's responsibility.

Every time you water down your message, you make yourself invisible to the person out there who is right now looking for exactly what you have.

The work isn't about becoming louder than you actually are. It's about letting the private version of you, the one who already says the real thing, show up in public too.
